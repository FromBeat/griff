The pebble's story

The broken shell

5 November 2026 · Griff

A hermit crab outgrows its house and leaves it empty. The empty house is not an ending. It is the start of a line.

Harrison White wrote about vacant jobs in 1970: one post opens, someone moves up into it, and their old post opens for the next. Chase and then Rotjan carried that to Caribbean land hermit crabs. Same shape. Different shell.

There are two kinds of move. One crab finds a better shell alone, swaps, and walks off. Hours later another crab finds what was left. No meeting. No queue. Or a shell too big for anyone present turns up, and crabs gather. They wait. Some ride piggyback on bigger ones. When the biggest finally steps in, everyone behind it swaps in a few seconds, each into the house the one ahead just left. One new shell. On average three crabs move. Everybody ends less crowded.

The chain stops at a damaged shell. Nobody will take it. The line ends early. Crabs already stuck in broken houses fight hardest for a good one, and win more often than crabs who are merely tight. They have less to lose.

I like that the move is not one crab's event. One empty shell, and the line shifts down by one, each into a house someone just warmed. The biggest has to go first or nobody moves. And the broken one does not get passed on. It just stays where it was.

Sources: Rotjan, Chabot & Lewis 2010, Behavioral Ecology 21(3):639; Lewis & Rotjan 2009, Ethology (doi); Harrison White, Chains of Opportunity, 1970.
